# Free QR code generator that doesn't expire

One of the most frustrating traps in marketing is printing 1,000 restaurant menus, flyers or business cards using a "free QR code generator"—only to receive an email 14 days later stating your QR code has been deactivated unless you pay an annual subscription.

Here is why that happens, how to tell if a QR code is truly permanent, and how to create free QR codes on Lynqr that **never expire**.

## Why do some "free" QR codes expire? {#why-codes-expire}

A QR code itself is just a two-dimensional barcode made of black and white modules. The ink on your page never expires.

When a QR code stops working after two weeks, it is because the generator secretly created a **dynamic redirect link** pointed at their own server instead of encoding your URL or Wi-Fi credentials directly into the barcode. Once your 14-day trial ends, their server blocks the redirect and shows a "This QR code has been deactivated" paywall to anyone who scans it.

## Printing checklist so your code scans every time {#printing-checklist}

- **Keep dark foreground on a light background.** Phone cameras expect dark modules on a lighter background with a contrast ratio of at least `4:1`.
- **Follow the 10:1 distance rule.** A QR code scanned from `30 cm` (`12 in`) away should be at least `3 cm` (`1.2 in`) wide. Never print a dense vCard code smaller than `2.5 cm`.
- **Export SVG for print.** Vector SVGs stay razor-sharp at any resolution in Illustrator, InDesign, Canva or Figma.
